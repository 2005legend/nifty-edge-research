# Trail Winners, Raise Hurdles, Trade Rarely

No intraday option-buying method, in India or anywhere else, has a verified live track record; the systematic methods that do have real-money or benchmark records are slow trend following (the SG Trend Index compounded **5.3% a year from 2000 to 2025**), option selling that harvests the volatility premium, and speed- or scale-based market making, while this system's own trigger, the noise-band breakout, rests on one 2024 working paper and harsher replications. Persistent winners are rare, since only **0.5% of Indian F&O traders active in all five years from FY22 to FY26 profited in every one of them**, and they sell options or trade futures, turn over little relative to their capital, trade on few days and close intraday; what a ₹10,000 option buyer can copy is the low turnover, the fixed size, exits committed at the moment of entry and same-day closing. Better timing cannot create an edge, but it can stop the system wasting one, and the largest modelled gain is in exits: a trail that starts once the index has moved one stop-distance in favour, a stop ratcheted up to VWAP, and time stops applied only to trades that never got going together beat the current fixed-target rules in **all 21 simulated cells**, by ₹20–170 a trade at the model's ₹750 stop (roughly half that at the ₹400 cap), at the price of a win rate near 21–30%. Entries should keep the 09:45 start and 15-minute checks, fire at the confirming check with a marketable limit and an index-level chase cap (resting pullback limits cut expectancy per signal by 35–110% because they fill on the failures), stop at 14:00 on non-expiry days and drop the 14:45 momentum check, while regime signals should raise the probability hurdle by at most eight points rather than switch the strategy off, and never act on its own recent P&L. The backdrop is sobering: the raw breakout edge on Nifty and Sensex spot was about **+5 bp a signal over 2023–26 and roughly zero in the Tuesday-expiry era**, and the 2026 daily proxy sits below its random-walk null, so the most likely simulation verdict remains "no edge that survives costs". The report closes with changes to the design spec, a registry of six core and up to five conditional trials inside the 10–15 budget, and six decisions for the owner, the weightiest being that trailing exits put the 50% win-rate goal out of reach by design.

## Proven records belong to trend followers, option sellers and machines

The methods with the strongest real-world proof are slow risk premia or businesses built on scale, and none of them can be traded by an intraday, one-lot, long-only option buyer. **Trend following** has the longest real-money record. AQR's century-long backtest across 67 markets earned a **net Sharpe ratio of 0.77**, and its authors advise planning for about 0.4 ([Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen](https://www.TrendFollowing.com/whitepaper/Century_Evidence_Trend_Following.pdf)). The live record is about half that: from 2000 to 2025 the SG Trend Index compounded **5.33% a year against 7.98% for the S&P 500 total return** ([Top Traders Unplugged](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-december-2025/)), and AQR's managed-futures fund has returned 4.16% a year since 2010 against 1.42% for T-bills ([AQR](https://funds.aqr.com/funds/aqr-managed-futures-strategy-fund)). The returns arrive in bursts, such as SG Trend's +27.3% in 2022 ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/trend-followers-turn-leaders-ctas-deliver-record-returns-2022/)), separated by flat years like 2023–25, and the edge lives at 1–12-month horizons. At the horizon that matters here the sign has flipped. Daily index autocorrelation across 20 markets turned negative in the 2000s as index futures and ETFs grew ([Baltussen, van Bekkum & Da](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/Indexing.pdf)), and the Sharpe ratio of a 5/20-day trend portfolio fell from **0.84 in 1995–2009 to 0.12 in 2009–2025** ([Kurth et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01550)). Trend cannot be the trigger of a weekly-option intraday system.

**Option selling** has the cleanest benchmark record, and it is the other side of this system's trades. Cboe's put-write index returned 10.1% a year against 9.8% for the S&P 500 from 1986 to 2015, with two-thirds of the volatility ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/performance-of-cboe-putwrite-indexes)), and 7–9% a year after 2010, mostly equity beta. Strip the beta out and little is left: the project's own calculation from Cboe's daily files shows the iron-condor benchmark CNDR returning **−0.1% a year over 2010–2025** before costs ([Cboe CNDR file](https://cdn-api.cboe.com/api/global/us_indices/daily_prices/CNDR_History.csv)). Buying is worse. Zero-beta at-the-money S&P straddles lose **about 3% a week** ([Coval & Shumway 2001](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/361566)), and protective puts are beaten by simply holding less equity ([Israelov, AQR](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/white-papers/pathetic-protection-the-elusive-benefits-of-protective-puts)). For this system the volatility premium is a cost to price, not a strategy to run, and a buyer's edge has to come from direction and timing strong enough to beat that premium plus costs over short holds.

**Clock and calendar effects decay fastest once they are published.** The S&P E-mini's 2–3 a.m. "overnight drift", which had earned over 60% of the index's return from 1998 to 2020, has **averaged about zero since 2021** ([Liberty Street Economics](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/)); the NightShares ETFs built to hold equities only overnight closed after about 14 months ([ETF.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/news/2-nightshares-etfs-close-after-struggling-gain-traction)); and the pre-FOMC drift died after 2015 ([Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v40y2021ics1544612320315956.html)). The calendar effects that lasted are the turn of the month, found in 31 of 35 countries ([McConnell & Xu](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v64y2008i2p49-64.html)) and in Nifty and Sensex through 2023 ([Review of Finance and Banking](https://rfb.ase.ro/Vol15-2023/dec2a.asp)), and the 1–5-day index reversal, which earned a gross Sharpe of 0.63 after 1999 in a sample that excluded India ([Baltussen, van Bekkum & Da](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/Indexing.pdf)). India's own overnight/intraday split persists: since 2015 Nifty has earned **+0.109% a day overnight and −0.069% a day from open to close** ([own data](<../research_notes/04_nifty_measured_facts.md>)), a standing headwind for intraday calls and a tailwind for puts.

**Published edges shrink, so plan for about half.** Across 97 predictors, returns were 26% lower out of sample and **58% lower after publication** ([McLean & Pontiff](https://ivey.uwo.ca/media/3775549/pontiff.pdf)); out-of-sample performance of 72 published strategies averaged about 50% of in-sample ([Falck, Rej & Thesmar](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/163650)); and the average US anomaly nets about **4 bp a month** after spreads and decay ([Chen & Velikov](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies/)). The US is the only one of 39 markets with a reliable post-publication decline ([Jacobs & Müller](https://fin.wiwi.uni-due.de/en/research/publications/anomalies-across-the-globe-once-public-no-longer-existent-14591)), which is a modest reason for hope in India, but Indian market-structure changes reset edges just as publication does. The consistent pattern is that edges survive when someone pays for them (crash insurance, hedging demand) or when a structural flow ignores publication, and die fastest when they are cheap clock rules on liquid index futures. The noise-band breakout sits at the weak end. It earned a Sharpe of 1.33 on SPY in its own paper ([Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/download)), but a related QQQ opening-range breakout fell from 1.06 to **0.23 once slippage was added** ([Brusco replication](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)), a five-market replication was indistinguishable from zero after costs ([MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)), and intraday momentum in SPY vanished after publication ([Rosa 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/fut.22375)).

Two planning rules follow. The project's earlier working edge, a per-trade Sharpe of about 0.13, implies an annual Sharpe of 1.0–1.6 at 60–150 trades a year, two to four times the live Sharpe of the best-proven systematic methods, so the design should plan around a **probability uplift of 2–4 points above no-edge odds rather than 6 or more** and treat any backtest with an annual Sharpe above 1.5 as overfit until live results say otherwise. And the breakout's post-publication Nifty sample, from mid-2024 onward, should decide its fate: a trigger whose post-publication edge is less than half its earlier edge is demoted even if the full sample passes.

### Persistent winners trade less, sell more and pre-commit their exits

SEBI's August 2026 studies show apparent skill evaporating with the horizon. **12.3%** of individual F&O traders were profitable in FY26, 9.0% over FY25–26 and 4.4% over FY22–26 cumulatively, and among traders active in all five years only **0.5%** made money in every one ([SEBI profitability study](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf); [SEBI behaviour study](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)). The behaviour study's 5,050-trader sample classes 93% of individuals as pure option buyers; **89.9% of them lost money, with a median return on peak margin of −114%**, against 43.8% losers among majority option sellers, although the sellers carried the tail, with losing sellers down ₹51.7 lakh on average among only 105 in the sample. Turnover relative to capital is the sharpest dividing line: traders running ₹10 lakh–1 crore of turnover on ₹10,000–1 lakh of margin were **94–98% loss-makers**, and traders active on more than 100 days were 42% of the population but carried 87% of the losses ([SEBI behaviour study](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)). Costs took **35% of losers' gross losses but only 21% of winners' gross profits** ([SEBI profitability study](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)).

Exchange data elsewhere agree. Fewer than 1% of Taiwanese day traders predictably earn positive returns after fees ([Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)); **97%** of Brazilians who day-traded mini-index futures on more than 300 days lost money ([Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:fgv:eesptd:525)); and in Korea selling volatility was the most profitable style for retail and institutions alike ([Hu, Kirilova, Park & Ryu](https://pure.skku.edu/en/publications/who-profits-from-trading-options/)). India's consistent winners are institutional, with 99% of prop and FPI gross profit coming from algorithmic entities, yet individuals classed as "algo" accounts lost ₹51,728 crore gross over FY25–26, so automation by itself is not an edge ([SEBI profitability study](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)). The behavioural evidence names specific habits. On NSE investor-level data, **day-trading P&L in index options is "flat to weakly positive" while positions not closed the same day lose significantly**, and when the 2015 lot-size increase squeezed small traders they moved further out of the money and shorter in maturity, and their returns fell 1.51% ([Agarwal, Ghosh, Prabhala & Zhao](https://www.fma.org/assets/docs/Derivatives2025/Zhao.pdf)). In a randomised experiment, **automatic stop-loss and take-gain orders reduced the disposition effect while reminders did not** ([Fischbacher, Hoffmann & Schudy](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/6576ae2f-bc9b-452c-9a2c-0379f8654c9d)); a US leverage cap cut high-leverage traders' losses by 40% ([Heimer & Simsek](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v132y2019i3p1-21.html)); CBOT proprietary traders who lost in the morning took above-average risk in the afternoon ([Coval & Shumway 2005](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v60y2005i1p1-34.html)); and measures of trading discipline predicted floor traders' later success ([Locke & Mann](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2004.01.004)).

No popular Indian retail strategy, from the 9:20 straddle and expiry strangles to OI and PCR systems and algo-marketplace products, has a verifiable live record. SEBI's return-verification agency (PaRRVA) went live in May 2026 with no verified results yet public ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/sebi-launches-return-verification-framework-parrva-to-curb-fake-performance-claims-125120800842_1.html)), and where enforcement exposed a trainer's real P&L it contradicted the marketing: "Baap of Chart" claimed 95% accuracy while running a **net trading loss of ₹2.89 crore** ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2023/10/223-finfluencer-baap-of-chart-fine-rs-17-2-crore-sebi/)). What this system can copy is narrow but concrete. One round trip turns over roughly ₹3,000–8,000 of premium (own arithmetic), so 150 trades a year means ₹4–12 lakh of turnover on ₹10,000 of capital, at or near SEBI's worst cell; low turnover relative to capital is therefore the first habit to adopt. The others are fixed small size, exit orders placed at entry rather than reminders, never converting a losing day trade into an overnight hold, never drifting to cheaper strikes when capital tightens, and a specialised two-sided book of calls and puts on two indices. All of these are associations or laboratory results that remove known ways to lose; none creates an edge.

## Entries: keep the 09:45–14:00 clock and stop resting limit orders

Units matter here. At the 16 October 2026 close (Nifty 23,146 and Sensex 74,729 in the project's [cached Yahoo bars](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/fetch_intraday_yf.py>)), one basis point of the index is worth about **₹75 a lot for an at-the-money option at delta 0.5** in either contract (own arithmetic). The ₹400 risk budget is therefore only about 5 bp of index at delta 0.5, and about 8 bp at the strike picker's 0.25 delta floor once roughly ₹100 of fees, spreads, overshoot and theta are set aside, so entry slippage and delay are first-order issues rather than details.

### The session's shape fixes the window, not the signal

Indian index volatility and volume are U-shaped, as documented for Nifty futures ([Sampath & Garg](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/irfi.12175); [Singh & Gangwar](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/89689/)) and confirmed in the project's 722 hourly sessions from November 2023 to October 2026, where the 09:15 bar on non-expiry days moves 26.3 bp on average against 14.1–14.6 bp at midday ([own measurement, A1](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/entry_timing_measurements_output.txt>)). The opening hour also carries the session's only significant drift, **−3.7 bp (t = −2.8) after a +5.9 bp average gap**, which is the intraday headwind in concentrated form; it sets the day's high on 39% of days and the low on 41%, and neither the opening-hour nor the opening-30-minute move reliably continues or reverses ([own measurement, A2/A4/A10](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/entry_timing_measurements_output.txt>)). International evidence shows option spreads widest just after the open ([Chan, Chung & Johnson](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v30y1995i03p329-346_00.html)), no Indian study of weekly-option spreads by time of day exists, and India VIX's first prints are noisy, so **09:45 remains a sound earliest entry**. Implied-volatility timing is second-order: India VIX peaks around 10:15 (median +0.63% against the previous close) and drifts lower into the close, falling 1.05% by 15:15 on Nifty expiry days ([own measurement, A9](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/entry_timing_measurements_output.txt>)), a swing worth only about ₹30–65 a lot at VIX 12–15. Short-dated option prices already embed the U-shaped volatility pattern ([Todorov & Zhang](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/todorov/htm/papers/odp.pdf)), so no clock hour is "cheap" relative to the movement it buys. Expiry days differ mainly late in the session: Nifty expiry Tuesdays are livelier only from 14:15, while Sensex expiry Thursdays ran markedly more volatile all afternoon in the recent 5-minute sample of 13 expiries ([own measurement, B1](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/entry_timing_measurements_output.txt>)).

The midday lull is a pace problem rather than a signal problem, and the binding constraint late in the day is the room left to run.

The per-hour signal column is noise. With a per-signal standard deviation near 55 bp, a 30-signal bucket has a standard error of about 10 bp, and the most recent 60 sessions rank 09:45–10:15 best instead. The room column, however, declines steadily: at 14:15 only 55% of days offer even a 20 bp excursion in either direction, and a 14:30 entry has just 40 minutes before the 15:10 exit. **The last non-expiry entry should move forward to 14:00, with 13:30 kept on expiry days.** There should be no lunch block; instead, an entry should require that the expected remaining excursion to the deadline, computed from remaining-session volatility, is at least twice the target distance plus the theta drag, with that multiplier fixed in advance. **The 14:45 rest-of-day momentum check has no support in recent data**: following the move from the previous close to 14:15 into 15:15 earned +0.3 bp (t = 0.3) over 722 sessions, and −1.7 bp for Nifty and −3.8 bp for Sensex in the 53 sessions since the closing auction began ([own measurement, A3b](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/entry_timing_measurements_output.txt>)). Since 3 August 2026 the spot index after 15:15 is an auction artefact. Nifty spot shows a median of two distinct 1-minute closes between 15:16 and 15:29, while Sensex keeps printing indicative values and its expiry-day 15:15 block averaged a 53 bp range ([own measurement, B1/C1](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/entry_timing_measurements_output.txt>)); on 3 September 2026 an indicative Sensex fall of about 2.5% sent some put premiums up 400–500% ([MarketsEasy](https://marketseasy.in/blog/sebi-expiry-settlement-price-cas-review-2026)). **No spot print after 15:15 should feed a signal, a label or a "close".**

### Resting limits fill on the breakouts that fail

The entry technique matters more than the entry hour. A resting limit order fills when adverse information arrives and misses when favourable news moves the price away ([Handa & Schwartz](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v51y1996i5p1835-61.html)), and that adverse selection mechanically produces poor post-trade returns ([Linnainmaa](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v65y2010i4p1473-1506.html)). The project's tests on the first noise-band signal of each day show the effect at full strength.

| Entry rule on a band breakout | Nifty fill rate | Nifty bp per signal | Sensex bp per signal | Recent 45 sessions, 15-min checks: Nifty / Sensex |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marketable order at the confirming check | 100% | **+5.36** (t 1.88) | **+5.57** (t 1.98) | +15.2 / +14.1 |
| Limit 0.25 noise units back | 83% | +3.10 | +3.45 | — |
| Limit 0.5 noise units back | 65% | +1.84 | +2.54 | — |
| Limit at the band edge (retest) | 64% | −0.60 | +1.27 | +5.7 / +9.0 |
| Wait one more check for confirmation | 77% kept | +3.16 vs +5.97 on the same days | +3.56 vs +5.26 | +14.6 vs +15.2 / +17.8 vs +14.1 |

Source: [own measurement, A6/B2](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/entry_timing_measurements_output.txt>). Unfilled limits count as zero.

The limits that never filled were the trades that ran, averaging **+36 to +49 bp** from a market entry (t = 6.8–9.3), so a pullback limit turns a momentum signal into a mean-reversion bet. The 15-minute close outside the band already serves as confirmation; waiting a further check screens out the 22–23% of breakouts that fail (averaging −18 to −23 bp) but gives that gain back through worse entry prices. Two spec items follow. The H2 pullback variant should go, and the order rule "limit at mid + 1 tick, never chased more than 2 ticks" should be replaced, because it is a resting limit in a fast market: one Nifty point moves an at-the-money premium by about ₹0.5, which is ten ticks, so the rule will often leave the order unfilled on exactly the winners. The replacement is a **marketable limit at the ask plus at most two ticks**, with the chase capped on the index rather than on the premium and stop fit re-checked at the actual fill.

### Delay costs most on the breakouts that work

Unconditionally the index is close to a random walk at the minute scale, with no significant continuation 1–10 minutes after 15-minute moves (|t| ≤ 1.4) and a faint reversal around 5 minutes ([own measurement, B3/C1](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/entry_timing_measurements_output.txt>)), consistent with Indian evidence that order-imbalance predictability fades within 5–30 minutes ([Tripathi, Dixit & Vipul](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612320316779)). On breakout signals, however, the drift runs with the signal and waiting costs money.

| Execution choice | Nifty extra entry distance (mean / median) | Sensex (mean / median) | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15-minute checks vs continuous 5-minute monitoring | +3.6 / +0.7 bp | +3.0 / 0.0 bp | Nifty outcome to 15:10 barely changes (+15.2 vs +15.4 bp) |
| 30-minute checks | +9.8 / +5.9 bp | +8.1 / +3.6 bp | Nifty outcome falls to +10.3 bp |
| 1-minute alert-to-fill delay | +2.7 bp mean | +0.6 bp mean | ≈ ₹200 a lot on Nifty at delta 0.5 |
| 3-minute delay | +4.3 bp | +1.1 bp | ≈ ₹80–320 a lot |
| 5-minute delay | +5.3 bp | +0.6 bp | Can exhaust the ₹400 budget on Nifty |

Source: [own measurement, B2/C1](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/entry_timing_measurements_output.txt>). The delay rows rest on 15–17 signals in one trending month and are upper-end estimates; across the 45-session sample the average cost of waiting is nearer ₹25–30 a lot per minute, with a fat right tail made of the fast breakouts that are also the winners.

Fifteen-minute checks are the right grid: they cost about 1 bp at the median against continuous monitoring, 5-minute checks added nothing to outcomes, and the SPY paper chose an even coarser half-hour grid explicitly to curb overtrading ([Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/download)). Delay damages stop fit before direction, since with a stop of 5–8 bp a few points of chase turn a valid alert into an over-budget trade, and single-bar delays can matter enormously: on Nasdaq micro futures one 15-minute delay flipped a surviving signal's out-of-sample t-statistic from +4.30 to −2.78 ([Mesfin 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04004)). The alert design should therefore tolerate a one-to-three-minute human delay without letting it grow. A "setup forming" pre-alert at 5-minute checks, sent when price is within 0.25 noise units of the band on the VWAP side, puts the owner at the screen before the confirming check. The signal alert then carries the side, the signal level S, the structural stop, the recommended strike with its maximum premium, and an index chase cap of S ± min(0.15σ(τ)·Open, 0.3·|S − stop|). It stays valid while the index is inside that cap and the live stop-fit check still passes for some strike in the 0.25–0.55 delta window, and it lapses after five minutes or at the next check, whichever comes first. The order is a marketable limit, re-priced once within the cap and then abandoned, and the backtest must simulate this whole policy at delays of 0, 1, 3 and 5 minutes with skipped signals scored as zero. Real manual latency is unknown, because no published study measures it, so the paper phase must timestamp the alert sent, the alert opened, the order placed and the fill, together with the index level at each.

### Filters earn feature status at most, and the recent era shows no raw edge

Contraction setups do not predict expansion on Nifty. Over 3,114 days the next day's range relative to its 20-day average was 0.94 after NR7 days, 1.01 after inside days and 1.03 after Bollinger squeezes, against 1.02 unconditionally, and the apparent "expansion" after an NR7 day is simply mean reversion from its own 0.57× range ([own measurement, D1](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/entry_timing_measurements_output.txt>)). As conditions on the breakout signal, a prior NR7 day went with better outcomes (Nifty +13.2 bp, N = 52; Sensex +19.4 bp, N = 41) and a prior inside day with worse ones (−0.8 and −3.3 bp), the same direction the SPY paper found ([Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/download)), but 40–50 events per index cannot carry a rule. Prior RSI(5) split the Nifty results in the opposite direction to SPY and should not be used. Alignment with the daily trend moved results by less than one standard error, and Nifty's open-to-close drift is about −7 bp whether the prior close sits above or below its 20- or 50-day average ([own measurement, A7/A10/D2](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/entry_timing_measurements_output.txt>)), so the day card can display trend alignment but should not veto a signal with it. Short signals did better than long ones on both indices (Nifty +7.5 vs +2.9 bp), consistent with the negative intraday drift, and a call/put dummy in the meta-model prices that asymmetry for one parameter. The period split matters more than any filter, because it shows the raw edge collapsing in the era the system will trade.

## Exits move more rupees than any entry rule

Exit rules cannot create expectancy from nothing, because for a price with no drift no bounded exit rule changes expected gross P&L; exits only trade win rate against payoff size. The project's Monte Carlo, which runs 40,000 paired one-minute Brownian paths per cell through Black-76 on a business-time clock across three entry cases and seven post-entry regimes, shows that with no drift **every one of the 20 exit policies tested loses ₹109–189 a trade** at a ₹750 stop, the cost of theta, spreads and fees ([project Monte Carlo](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/exit_rules_mc_output.txt>)). Stops follow the same logic: under a random walk they always reduce expected return and help only when returns keep trending after the stop is hit ([Kaminski & Lo](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf)), and tight stops underperformed after costs on US stocks ([Lo & Remorov](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/107017)). This system's stop sits **inside the breakout's own noise**, since the median maximum adverse excursion of Nifty band breakouts was −21 bp against a stop of 5–8 bp ([own measurement, A5](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/entry_timing_measurements_output.txt>)). It works as a test of whether the timing is right over the next 20 minutes, which is tolerable only because the ₹400 cap forces it, and it is the strongest reason to gate entries to momentum regimes, where dealer short-gamma hedging makes tight stops stop hurting ([Dim, Eraker & Vilkov](https://dx.doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4692190)). The primary stop belongs on the **index**, evaluated on 1-minute closes. A premium stop at the same rupee distance behaves like an index stop plus a hidden time stop, because theta drags the premium to the trigger, and it lost money whenever drift was favourable. In the model the realised loss at the stop averaged **about 1.3× the nominal delta loss** once overshoot, three half-spreads, fees and theta were counted, and the stop-fit check must budget for that.

### A trail plus a VWAP ratchet beat fixed targets in all 21 modelled cells

For momentum entries, theory and evidence agree that winners should not be capped. With positive drift it is optimal never to trail at all and simply hold ([Glynn & Iglehart](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.41.6.1096)); under mean reversion it is optimal to pair a take-profit with the trailing stop ([Leung & Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.03960)); so the drift after entry decides which applies. In the SPY noise-band study, a stop at the opposite band edge gave a **Sharpe of 0.61** while a trailing stop at the larger of the current band and VWAP gave **1.24**, with a trade hit rate of only 37% and no profit target ([Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/download)). The project's Monte Carlo puts rupee numbers on the trade-off.

Only two of the 20 policies tested never lose to the current rules: E-trail, which gains ₹20–170 a trade (about **+0.03R to +0.23R**, averaging ₹61), and a tighter 1.0b trail, which gains ₹14–88 (averaging ₹46). E-trail has the higher floor and average, so it is the robust default. Its win rate sits at 21–30% in every cell except failed breakouts and quiet no-drift days, with average wins of 2.4–4.6× average losses, against 1.5–1.9× under the current rules, whose theta and time exits already compress the nominal 2.5× target. The VWAP runner has the largest upside under trend but gives back up to ₹217 on failed breakouts, and its 1–20% win rate would make any live verdict nearly impossible. A breakeven move should not be added. Breakeven on the index is not breakeven on the premium, since exiting at the entry level still loses theta, fees and spreads worth about ₹100–200; the rule cuts expectancy whenever drift persists and turns normal pullbacks into scratch trades that still lose money. Trailing exits make discipline harder, because they require repeated actions while in profit, exactly when the disposition effect pulls toward selling early ([Odean](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/disposition/disposition.html)), so any exit before the trail is hit must be logged as an override.

### Time stops belong on laggards only

At a 0.10% stop and a 0.25% target, a driftless trade resolves in about 22 minutes at normal volatility and 46 minutes at low volatility (E[τ] = a·b/σ²), so the current 45-minute stall exit and 90-minute deadline bind on only 9–10% of trades in normal conditions and 25–28% in quiet ones. They are cheap insurance, costing ₹10–23 a trade when drift persists and saving ₹40–90 when the edge is absent, the breakout fails or volatility is low ([project Monte Carlo](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/exit_rules_mc_output.txt>)). Applying them to every trade cut trailing runners and gave up about ₹100–120 a trade under low-volatility drift, while applying them only to **laggards**, trades that never reached +1 stop-distance, kept the upside. The rule is to exit after 30 minutes if the index is within ±0.05% of entry and after 60 minutes if the trade has never reached +1b, while trades already trailing are managed only by the trail and the hard exit. Holding times of 60–75 minutes were what let the two surviving signals in the Nasdaq falsification study clear their costs ([Mesfin 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04004)).

### Option mechanics set the hard edges

SL-M orders on index options are banned on NSE and BSE, so the broker-side backstop must be an SL-limit order with its limit set well beyond the trigger ([Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/marketintel/bulletin/305785/stop-loss-market-sl-m-orders-blocked-for-index-options)). Its trigger sits at entry − (₹400 + slippage)/lot with the limit a further max(10% of premium, 5 ticks) below; it should be placed within 30 seconds of the fill and ratcheted to the premium equivalent of the trailing index level every 5–10 minutes, so that a hesitating trader still has a resting order, and a breached backstop that does not fill should trigger an immediate marketable exit from the alert. Hard exits stay at **15:00 on expiry days and 15:10 otherwise**, both before the auction freeze, since the 15:00–15:15 VWAP sets the auction's reference price and US 0DTE effective spreads roughly double in the final hour, to 10.5% of mid ([Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)). One exit is new: **close any open trade 5 minutes before a scheduled intraday announcement**. On RBI decision days India VIX fell 1.95% on the day (t = −3.81), and on Budget days it fell 9.3% even though the range ran 2.1× implied ([own macro checks](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/macro_crossasset_checks_output.txt>)), so post-event IV crush works against a long option even when its direction is right.

### The data cannot pick the winner, so pre-commit

Exit parameters are among the easiest things to overfit. With five years of daily data no more than about 45 variations should ever be tried, and an overfit rule's expected out-of-sample return is negative rather than zero ([Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf)); one study searched 8,960 exit configurations and then saw a chronological hold-out Sharpe as low as −5 ([Li, Laryea & Ihlamur](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27150)). Power is the binding problem. The paired per-trade difference between two exit policies has a standard deviation of about ₹900–1,500, so detecting a ₹60 improvement at 80% power needs about **2,600 setups** and ₹100 about 950, while the modelled E-trail advantage in realistic weak-drift cells is ₹20–65. The roughly 2,000-setup backtest will therefore most likely fail to separate the policies, and live trading cannot separate them at all. The defensible procedure fixes the barriers in advance in R-multiples (trail activation at +1.0b, trail distance 1.5b, a 60-minute laggard deadline), spends only two trials, the current exits against E-trail paired on identical setups, and pre-commits the decision: adopt E-trail unless it trails the current rules with t ≤ −1 on 2019–23 or is negative on the 2024–25 walk-forward by more than one standard error. Adopting it also changes the scoreboard. The payoff is no longer binary, the expected win rate falls to about 21–30%, the decision log's 40% breakeven no longer applies, and the SPRT and CUSUM kill-switches must be re-specified on **mean net R per trade**, with the variance estimated in simulation.

## Regimes earn a hurdle of at most eight points, never a switch

The literature says intraday momentum and breakouts pay most in volatile, stressed, dealer-short-gamma markets. The SPY noise-band strategy's Sharpe rose from about 1.5 on all days to about 3.5 when VIX was above 40, and its losing years were the calm 2016 (−12.8%) and 2017 (−6.9%) ([Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/download)). Last-half-hour momentum existed only when dealers were net short gamma, with an R² of 3.58% against 0.05% ([Baltussen et al. 2021](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). The documented failure mode is panic followed by rebound: momentum crashes come after declines with high volatility, when markets snap back, and a forecast-based dynamic version roughly doubles momentum's Sharpe out of sample across markets ([Daniel & Moskowitz](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)), while intraday momentum lost significance during the COVID shock in both the S&P 500 and the CSI 300 ([Hossain et al.](https://dspace5old.zcu.cz/handle/11025/46501?locale=en)).

### Nifty breakouts pay more rupees in volatility, but not more per unit of noise

The project tested this on Nifty with a daily OHLC proxy of the breakout from 2015 to 5 October 2026, entering at the band, holding to the close and measuring in "noise units". The proxy has a mechanical bias, showing +0.12σ to +0.15σ a trade on simulated driftless days, so the real edge is the observed figure minus that null. The unconditional result was +0.224σ (t = 7.35) over 1,517 trades, an excess of about **+0.07σ to +0.11σ** ([own regime measurement](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/regime_timing_measure_output.txt>)). In noise units the edge did not vary significantly with any of 13 regime variables, none of whose top-versus-bottom spreads reached |t| > 2, but in index basis points it scaled strongly with volatility, from 6 bp a trade at India VIX below 12 to 26 bp at VIX of 25 or more. For a buyer facing fixed rupee costs that is the main regime channel, but theta and the volatility premium also scale with implied volatility, so the net benefit has to be measured in option P&L.

The system also contains a hidden regime filter that points the wrong way. The ₹400 stop fits only when the structural stop is within about 18 Nifty points (0.08%) at delta 0.25, while the day's noise unit averaged 35 bp at VIX below 12 but 133 bp at VIX of 25 or more. The stop-fit gate will therefore pass mostly on calm days, the days with the smallest absolute payoff, and the backtest should report stop-fit pass rates by VIX tercile, which costs no trials.

### Timing by the strategy's own P&L or by VIX level fails

The broader literature is cautious about strategy timing. Volatility-managed versions of 103 strategies had lower out-of-sample Sharpe ratios in **72** of them ([Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang & Yan](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)); after costs, volatility management of anything but the market portfolio adds nothing ([Barroso & Detzel](https://sbfc.sydney.edu.au/2019/papers/P057_Named.pdf)); and timing on valuation spreads proved "deceptively difficult" ([Asness et al., via CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=29629)). Timing survives when the state is slow-moving and well measured, as with factor timing that raised out-of-sample Sharpe ratios from 0.76 to 0.87–0.96 at monthly frequency before costs ([Haddad, Kozak & Santosh](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w26708/w26708.pdf)), but a personal system with at most 150 trades a year sits in the "few episodes, unstable relations" zone where switching does harm. Timing on the strategy's own record fails outright. Monthly proxy P&L had zero persistence (Spearman −0.005), and a filter that traded only while trailing 60-day P&L was positive **kept the worse trades** (+0.146σ kept against +0.367σ skipped, t = −2.34), in both 2015–19 and 2020–26 ([own regime measurement, C](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/regime_timing_measure_output.txt>)). That is what Kaminski and Lo predict when returns lack serial correlation, and it means the live CUSUM must be framed as a detector of a broken system, not a regime timer.

Every regime detector trades lag against false alarms. Across the eight Nifty drawdowns of 10% or more since 2015, an online two-state jump model with a fixed penalty of λ = 20 flagged each fall **10–28 trading days after the peak**, entered its bad state only 1.6 times a year with 5% of spells under five days, and cleared 23–161 days after the trough, which makes it slow in V-shaped rebounds. India VIX above 20 lagged by 6 to **182 days**, missed the slow FII-driven grinds of 2019 and 2024–25, and produced spells under five days 65% of the time ([own regime measurement, D](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/regime_timing_measure_output.txt>)). Level thresholds also drift: the share of days with VIX above 20 went from 70% in 2020 to 0% in 2023 and 2% in 2025, so a fixed-level gate is effectively a "2020–22 only" gate. Fat tails cut a Gaussian-calibrated variance CUSUM's false-alarm interval from about 2,700 to about 200 days. Regimes should therefore be read from self-normalising variables, with VIX rate of change for shocks, a fixed-λ jump model for slow trends ([Shu, Yu & Mulvey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05272)), and thresholds calibrated by bootstrap on Nifty's own returns.

### The hurdle layer

The regime layer outputs an additive margin Δ on the EV gate: the calibrated p̂ must reach the measured breakeven + 0.04 + Δ, with **Δ capped at +0.08**. Inputs are computed after the close and applied the next day, except the intraday shock check, and every component is logged on the day card.

| # | Component | Trigger, fixed in advance | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Hard off (unchanged) | Event gate E3, data or feed fault, live costs > 30% over model, CUSUM or SPRT stop, capital ≤ ₹7,000 | No alerts |
| 1 | Shock | ln(VIX t−1 / VIX t−6) ≥ +0.20, or a bootstrap-calibrated variance-CUSUM alarm (σ ×1.5, about one false alarm a year) in the last 20 sessions, or \|gap\| ≥ 1.5σ̂ from the HAR forecast | Δ += 0.04, both sides, for 5 sessions |
| 2 | Slow trend | Online jump model, λ = 20 fixed, Shu–Yu–Mulvey features, yearly expanding refit, 1-day lag; "bad" state | Δ += 0.04 for longs, 0.02 for shorts |
| 3 | Gamma (only after the gamma engine is validated) | Net gamma exposure ≥ 0 (dealers long gamma), sign convention fixed by the engine's own pre-registered test | Δ += 0.04 |
| 4 | Calibration shortfall | Beta posterior of observed minus predicted hit rate across all labelled setups, prior worth 100 setups, above 0.03 | Δ += the shortfall, capped at 0.06; never loosens |
| 5 | Macro stress score (spec) | Oil, rupee, yields, FII, geopolitics, 0–9 | Capped at +0.02 unless trial RV-1 passes; still used for range and display |
| — | Volatility level | VIX level or percentile | No hurdle; volatility enters through remaining-session sizing, the "implied move not rich" gate and EV |

Sources: [own regime measurement](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/regime_timing_measure_output.txt>); [Daniel & Moskowitz](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439); [Baltussen et al. 2021](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf).

At the last measurement on 5 October 2026 the jump model was in its bad state and the 5-day VIX change stood at +0.195, just under the shock threshold, so the layer would set Δ = +0.04 for longs and +0.02 for shorts, tilting against long breakouts as the 2026 evidence does. Hurdles beat switches because no regime cut in the data shows a negative edge with |t| ≥ 2, because switching off any volatility tercile would forgo positive expectancy per day (t = 2.9–3.7 in each), because lags of 10–28 days into a regime and 23–161 days out of it make binary switches wrong at turning points, and because a hurdle fails gracefully while the skipped setups stay labelled. A hurdle should be upgraded to a switch-off only if the H1 backtest shows negative net option expectancy in that regime with t ≤ −2 over at least 60 trades, in the direction the literature predicts. Switching back on follows the same logic: event and shock blocks lapse on the calendar, while a CUSUM, SPRT or annual-review stop sends the system to shadow mode until a fresh pre-registered check on labelled setups, not a run of good paper results, re-qualifies it. Three standing rules keep the layer honest. Refits run on a fixed schedule, with HAR monthly on a rolling 750 days, the jump model yearly with λ never re-tuned, and the meta-model every six months or 250 labelled setups, averaging expanding and three-year rolling fits. Monitoring runs in order of statistical weight, with monthly calibration-in-the-large on all labelled setups (about 23 a month) first, the CUSUM as the collapse detector, and regime-specific live hit rates shown for information only below 100 trades per regime. And market-structure breaks are treated like publication dates, with results reported separately before and after 20 November 2024, 1 September 2025 and 3 August 2026, since the post-reform period already shows twice the share of both-band whipsaw days (4.1% against 2.0% before COVID).

Several items leave the registry. The three payoff shapes give way to the X0/X1 pair, the H2 pullback is dropped, the gradient-boosting challenger is deferred until about 300 live labelled setups exist, and the three gamma sign conventions become one. Band-width, check-grid, earliest-entry and cutoff variants are adopted a priori or not at all, NR7, inside-day and trend-alignment filters are shadow-computed only, and VIX-level, vol-of-vol, 200-DMA, ADX, Brent and rupee gates are excluded because their tests came out null or reversed, as are equity-curve switches, re-tuning of λ and any grid over trail, target or time-stop parameters. The spec's pass gates still apply with three additions. A regime add-on must not lower net P&L per available day, must raise per-trade expectancy in the predicted direction with t ≥ 1, and must improve in at least 4 of 6 out-of-sample years with at least 30 trades per fold. Any future entry-timing trial must raise net rupees per signal in at least 4 of 6 years and on both indices, with a paired gain above two standard errors and a stated mechanism. And every registry row records its cumulative trial count. Six pieces of work cost no trials because they rank no P&L, and they should run first: stop-fit, strike-picker and trigger pass rates by regime; maximum favourable and adverse excursion paths with time to maximum favourable excursion, on the 2019–22 training window only; the gamma engine's validation against realised volatility; the Nifty daily AR(1) and MAC(5) estimate; the OI-wall test against settlement; and the option-depth and latency logs.

## Six decisions only the owner can make

Each decision below follows the project's standing rule of stating what matters, the options, a recommendation and what cannot yet be known, and each should be recorded with its reason in `docs/decisions.md` once the owner chooses; this report does not edit that file.

### D1. Exit design, and retiring the 50% win-rate goal

**What matters.** On 18 October the owner asked to improve the system until its win rate crosses 50%, and the best-supported exit design moves the other way. E-trail lowers the modelled win rate to about 21–30% (from 28–39% under the current rules, outside failed-breakout and quiet no-drift cells) while raising expected value by ₹20–170 a trade at model scale in every modelled regime. The owner's "net target ≥ 1.5× the loss" survives as a planning constraint on strike choice and as average win ÷ average loss, which ran 2.4–4.6× under E-trail against 1.5–1.9× under the current rules, but it is no longer a promise on every winning trade. Power is low: the backtest can resolve only exit differences of ₹75–100 a trade or more, while the modelled gap in realistic weak-drift cells is ₹20–65, so the most likely backtest outcome is "indistinguishable" and the choice rests on theory and robustness.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Cost and time |
|---|---|---|---|
| (1) X0 vs X1, with X1 the a priori default | Robust in all 21 modelled cells; consistent with theory; 2 trials | Rests on a model rather than Nifty data; needs repeated manual trail actions | No extra time |
| (2) Add X2, the VWAP runner | Tests the fat-tail mechanism behind breakout returns | 1–20% win rates make paper and live verdicts nearly impossible; long losing streaks; a third trial | Same |
| (3) Keep fixed targets only | Higher win rate (about 30–39%); simpler execution | Forgoes ₹20–170 a trade; loses to E-trail in every cell | Saves one trial |

**Recommendation.** Option 1, retiring the 50% win-rate goal in favour of net R per trade and re-specifying the SPRT and CUSUM on mean R. This would change if the zero-trial excursion pilot shows the project's setups reaching their maximum favourable excursion within about 30 minutes and then reverting, in which case a fixed target near 2×, the winner on failed breakouts, becomes the default.

**What can't be known yet.** The drift profile of the project's own entries, real slippage on manual trailing exits, and whether Angel One supports index-triggered or trailing orders on options. The pilot is the excursion study on the 2019–22 training window, which ranks no exit P&L.

### D2. Stop geometry under the ₹400 risk unit

**What matters.** ₹400 at the stop, less about ₹100 of fees, spreads, overshoot and theta, leaves about ₹300 of delta loss, which at the 0.25 delta floor allows a stop of about **18 Nifty points or 60 Sensex points, roughly 0.08% of the index**. Breakouts' median adverse excursion is about 0.21%, and the band's noise unit runs from 35 bp in calm markets to 133 bp in stressed ones, so stop fit will pass mostly when the band edge happens to be very close and mostly on calm days whose payoff is smallest. The likely result is few eligible setups, concentrated where the edge is thinnest, and possibly fewer than the 30 trades per fold the spec's pass gate requires.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Cost and time |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Keep ₹400 and accept rare alerts | Matches the 4%-of-capital decision; no new money | May starve the backtest; biased toward calm regimes | None |
| (b) Raise the account to ₹15,000 before go-live and risk ₹600 (still 4%) | Stop room rises to about 0.13% at delta 0.25, so more natural invalidation levels fit | ₹5,000 more at risk before any edge is shown | Capital, not time |
| (c) Lower the delta floor to 0.20 | More trades fit the budget | Fixed costs dominate; expected value was negative at every tested edge below delta 0.2 in the earlier strike simulation ([pricing note](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/option_trade_construction.md>)) | Not recommended |

**Recommendation.** Option (a) for the simulation, with the zero-trial pilot reporting stop-fit pass rates by VIX tercile and eligible setups per year. If eligible setups fall below about 60 a year, or the pass rate in the high-VIX tercile is under half the low-VIX rate, choose between (b) and alert-off before go-live. Raising capital before go-live is a design choice, not the after-a-drawdown top-up that the SEBI evidence warns against.

**What can't be known yet.** The pass rates themselves, which the pilot measures without viewing any P&L.

### D3. Trade frequency

**What matters.** A sequential go/no-go verdict needs about 95–115 trades on average ([earlier validation calculation](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/signal_fusion_validation.md>)), about 8 months at 3 trades a week and about 12 months at 2. Against that, 150 trades a year put roughly ₹4–12 lakh of premium turnover on ₹10,000 of capital, at or inside SEBI's 94–98%-loss cell; this is an association, not proof that frequency harms a rules-gated trader, and if SEBI counts only one side of premium turnover the thresholds double. In practice the raw breakout fires on about half of all days, about 2.6 times a week before any gate ([own measurement, A5](<../research_notes/Nifty options strategy timing refinement/entry_timing_measurements_output.txt>)), so the gates may hold live trades below two a week anyway.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Cost and time |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Keep up to 3 a week | Fastest verdict (about 8 months) | Sits in SEBI's worst turnover cell; most cost drag and streak exposure | — |
| (b) ≤ 100 live trade-days a year, pro-rata monthly, plus shadow labels | Turnover under ₹10 lakh; marginal setups shadowed rather than traded | Verdict in about 12 months | About 4 more months |
| (c) About 1 a week plus shadow labels | Lowest risk | Live verdict takes about 2 years | About 16 more months |

**Recommendation.** Option (b). Move to (a) if live costs come in at or below assumption and the first 40 trades show calibration within 5 points, and to (c) if the CUSUM trips within the first 30 trades.

**What can't be known yet.** Whether the SEBI frequency–loss link applies to a selective, rules-gated trader. The cheap pilot compares shadow outcomes of "would-have-been third trade of the week" setups with the first two.

### D4. Holdout integrity

**What matters.** The spec reserves 2026 for a single look, but this round's measurements already examined November 2023 to October 2026 on hourly, 5-minute and 1-minute spot data, plus the 2026 daily proxy, and several recommended rules drew partly on them: the 14:00 cutoff, dropping the pullback entry, and the long-side and slow-trend hurdles. The 2024–25 walk-forward and the 2026 holdout are therefore no longer untouched for the breakout family. A 2026 pass would overstate confidence, while a 2026 failure remains informative. The contamination is modest, because the measurements were descriptive and spot-only with no option P&L, but it is real.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Cost and time |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Keep 2026 as the holdout and disclose the contamination | No delay | The holdout's evidential weight is overstated | None |
| (b) Fold 2026 into the walk-forward and make the first ≈ 150 labelled setups after rules freeze the clean holdout | A genuinely unseen test | About 6–7 months at about 23 setups a month | Time |
| (c) Both: report 2026 as "touched", go live on simulation plus paper mechanics as the owner prefers, but claim a confirmed edge and scale beyond one lot only after the forward holdout | Keeps the owner's timeline and restores an honest test | A two-stage claim | Time, for scaling only |

**Recommendation.** Option (c). The question becomes moot if the simulation fails its gates outright.

**What can't be known yet.** How much the descriptive look biases the 2026 option-P&L result; it cannot be measured, only avoided from here on.

### D5. Trial allocation

**What matters.** On 600 trades the deflated Sharpe ratio falls from 0.947 at 10 trials to 0.869 at 30 ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf); [earlier calculation](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/signal_fusion_validation.md>)). Documented regime effects of about 5–8 win-rate points need 340–590 trades per regime to detect, so with the likely 300–600 H1 trades in total, power is 29–37%, and exits have similarly low power. The most likely outcome for every regime and exit variant is a point estimate in the expected direction with |t| < 2, and every trial spent on them raises the hurdle H1 itself must clear.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Cost and time |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Minimal: B0, X1, M1, RV-2 | Smallest deflation penalty | Slow-trend and gamma hurdles ride on priors alone | 4 trials |
| (b) The registry above: 6 core plus up to 5 conditional, hard stop at 13 | Each mechanism tested once; conditional slots unlocked only by earlier results | Still underpowered; uses most of the budget | 6–13 trials |
| (c) Every variant proposed in the source notes (about 25) | Looks thorough | Blows the budget; classic overfitting | Not recommended |

**Recommendation.** Option (b). Switch to (a) if H1 leaves fewer than four slots or fails on both B0 and X1, since the regime layer is moot without a trigger, and run the regime components as fixed priors with no trials if the backtest yields fewer than 200 H1 trades.

**What can't be known yet.** How many H1 trades survive the gates, which the zero-trial pass-rate pilot answers.

### D6. Loss-streak pauses versus equity-curve neutrality

**What matters.** The behavioural evidence on afternoon loss-chasing and blow-ups before quitting argues for pausing after a run of losses, while the statistical evidence argues against it: on Nifty, P&L-based filters skipped the better trades (t = −2.34), and stop rules help only with serially correlated returns. Both are partly right, because a pause protects against the owner's behaviour rather than the system's, and the spec's existing day and week limits barely bind at three trades a week.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Cost and time |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Spec locks only, plus SPRT, CUSUM and the annual review | No expectancy lost to timing | Relies on the one-trade-a-day lock and typed unlocks to curb tilt | None |
| (b) Live pauses after 2 straight losses, −2R in a week or −4R in a month | Strong behavioural guard | Forgoes setups that were better than average in the proxy; slows validation | Lost trades |
| (c) "Shadow pause": after those triggers, alerts continue as paper trades until the period ends | Guards behaviour while labels and calibration keep accruing | Live P&L forgone if the edge is real | Small |

**Recommendation.** Option (a), with (c) available whenever the owner feels tilt, invoked as a logged choice. This would change if the simulation finds positive serial correlation in the system's own trade outcomes, which would give (b) a statistical case as well.

**What can't be known yet.** The autocorrelation of trade outcomes, which costs no trials to compute in the simulation.

## Conclusion

The question this research began with, when to trade, turns out to be mostly a question about geometry and exits. The ₹400 risk unit forces stops of about 0.08% on breakouts that typically move about 0.21% against the entry at some point before the close, which makes stop fit rather than signal quality the system's de facto regime filter and points it toward the calm days when breakouts pay least. Entry-timing rules are worth a few basis points, exit design is worth roughly ₹10–85 a trade at the real ₹400 scale, and regime timing is worth only what a capped hurdle can deliver without spending trials the budget cannot afford. The recent era sharpens the stakes: with the raw breakout edge near zero on both indices under Tuesday expiries and the 2026 proxy below its random-walk null, a disciplined pipeline is more likely to conclude "alert-off" than to find a tradeable edge, and against an 87.7% loss base rate that is still a good outcome.

The practical implication is to spend the next weeks on zero-trial measurement before spending any trials. Stop-fit pass rates by regime, the excursion profile of the system's own setups, real alert-to-fill latency and option spreads by minute of day will decide whether E-trail is the right exit, whether ₹10,000 can host the strategy at all, and how many registry slots are worth using. Four gaps remain open: Angel One's support for index-triggered or trailing orders on options was not verified; Indian weekly-option spreads by time of day have never been measured; several key sources were read only at abstract level (Kaminski and Lo's full results, the Carr–López de Prado rule heat maps, the out-of-sample tables of the factor-timing studies); and every exit figure comes from a stylised Brownian model that must be re-run on real 1-minute futures paths before exits are frozen. This is research for a system the owner builds and runs, not investment advice or a recommendation to trade any contract.

# Trade Rarely, Price Precisely, Prove Slowly

A ₹5,000 alerts-only system for buying Nifty and Sensex weekly options can be made quant-grade only by reversing the draft's priorities. Direction should come from at most two price-based triggers that have a market mechanism behind them: a regime-gated noise-band breakout, and, once it passes a Nifty test, a thresholded rest-of-day momentum trade. A third, opening option-order flow, earns a vote only after months of live logging. Options-market, macro and sentiment data should decide whether to trade, when, which contract, and how big a move to expect, but never which way. The binding constraint is arithmetic, not signal choice. A ₹500 loss cap on a near-the-money lot forces an index stop of roughly 0.08–0.15% (18–34 Nifty points), and the index moves at least 0.11% against its open on about 85% of days. With no edge, the chance of hitting a target 2.3–2.6 times the stop first is therefore only 0.29–0.34, and the model has to lift it to about 0.40–0.45. That corresponds to roughly 60–65% hourly directional accuracy, while honest out-of-sample models reach 52–58% on daily index direction. Three of your planning assumptions fail the numbers. Risking ₹200–500 is 4–10% of capital, above full Kelly at plausible edges, and even a genuine 42% hit rate gives a 42% chance of halving the account within about 120 trades at ₹500 risk. Breakeven at realistic all-in costs of ₹65–135 per round trip is 40–46%, not 35–38%. And a 40-trade paper phase misjudges a real edge about one time in four. The debit and credit spreads that earlier designs fell back on when options are expensive need about ₹29,000 of exposure margin on the short leg, so they are unavailable at ₹5,000. A working system will win ₹800–1,000 on its good trades, average a few tens of rupees per trade, say "no trade" on most days, and need about 100 live trades (7–9 months at three a week) before it earns a statistical verdict.

## Only two price signals deserve a vote on direction

The starting odds are poor. SEBI's August 2026 study found that **87.7% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY26**, that options produced 92% of those losses, that 82.1% were losers even before costs, that the loss rate climbs with turnover to about 95%, and that option losses were "largely independent of market direction" ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)). Nifty's own history explains part of this. Since 2015 the index has gained **+0.109% a day overnight but lost −0.069% a day between open and close**, and only 46.7% of sessions close above their open ([own data](<../research_notes/04_nifty_measured_facts.md>)). Global cues set the gap and nothing after it. Regressing Nifty's open-to-close move on the prior US session, Brent, USD/INR and Nifty's own prior day gives **R² of 0.002 over 2015–2026 and 0.003 in 2026** ([own data](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/macro_crossasset_checks_output.txt>)). Nothing in the four signal families reaches grade A (peer-reviewed, Indian, surviving costs) for intraday direction. So the design question is less "which signals predict?" than "which honest job can each signal do?" The evidence supports five jobs: **direction**, **regime** (trend day or chop day), **volatility** (how far the index will move, which sets stops and targets), **sizing** (with one lot, the bar a trade must clear), and **stand-aside**.

### Technical signals survive only with a regime gate and a mechanism

The breakout idea in your H1 is the right core, but the evidence attaches strict conditions to it. Zarattini, Aziz and Barbon's "noise area" defines a band around the open (or the prior close, after a gap) as the 14-day average absolute move from the open at each minute of the day. It checks on the half-hour, trails with VWAP and goes flat at the close. On SPY over 2007–2024 it earned a **Sharpe ratio of 1.33 with a hit rate of only about 43%**, and it did better when VIX was above 20 ([Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/download)). Independent replications are harsher. A replication of the related QQQ opening-range breakout fell from a **Sharpe of 1.06 to 0.23 once 2¢ entry and 4¢ stop slippage were added**, and 76% of its filtered profit came from 2022 alone ([Brusco replication](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)). A 2026 walk-forward falsification of 14 retail intraday signal families on Nasdaq micro futures found that none survived friction. The only survivors were Gaussian-mixture regime signals held 60–75 minutes, and even those came after more than 53 parameter combinations had been tried. **Delaying one of them by a single 15-minute bar flipped its out-of-sample t-statistic from +4.30 to −2.78** ([Mesfin 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04004)). That last result matters for an alert system whose orders are placed by hand 30–90 seconds after the signal. Latency has to be inside the backtest, and any signal whose sign flips with a three-minute delay should be rejected.

The late-day trade needs rebuilding rather than tuning. Across more than 60 futures markets from 1974 to 2020, the return from the prior close to 30 minutes before today's close predicted the last half-hour with an **out-of-sample R² of +2.88%**. The draft's first-half-hour version had an out-of-sample R² of **−1.71%** in the pooled equity sample. The effect lived almost entirely on days when option dealers were net short gamma (R² 3.58% against 0.05%), and when the first-half-hour and rest-of-day returns agreed, daily success rose from 55% to 61% before costs ([Baltussen et al. 2021](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). The warnings are just as specific. For SPY the predictability "disappears in the out-of-sample period" after publication, although thresholded versions beat always-on ones ([Rosa 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/fut.22375)). Plain intraday momentum is insignificant in Korea ([Kang, Kang & Lee 2021](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/fut.22301)). The only Indian study, a practitioner paper, is unreadable ([Motwani et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383567351_Hedging_Demand_and_Intraday_Momentum_within_the_Indian_Stock_Market)). This setup is therefore a hypothesis to test on Nifty before it touches money, not a component to deploy.

Several popular technical tools fall away. Fast trend has collapsed: an exponentially weighted 5/20-day trend portfolio across about 100 futures went from a **Sharpe of 0.84 in 1995–2009 to 0.12 in 2009–2025**, with equity indices among the worst hit ([Kurth et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01550)). Nifty, with a ₹0.05 tick on an index in the tens of thousands, is exactly the kind of small-tick contract that lost its trend edge. The 50-day average survives only as a volatility flag, because five-day moves are 54% larger below it ([own data](<../research_notes/04_nifty_measured_facts.md>)). Order-flow imbalance explains same-minute prices very well, with an out-of-sample R² of 82% using five book levels, but its out-of-sample R² for the *next* minute is negative ([Cont, Cucuringu & Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.13213)). On the NSE its predictive power is strong for about five minutes and gone within thirty ([Tripathi, Dixit & Vipul 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612320316779)). With manual execution it can only veto an alert at the moment it fires. Support and resistance has real evidence only for round numbers and recent extremes. Take-profit orders cluster at round numbers and stop-losses just beyond them, so trends tend to stall at a round level and accelerate once it breaks ([Osler 2003](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1540-6261.00588)). No rigorous evidence supports VWAP or floor pivots as levels, so they belong in trade management, not in entries.

The volatility engine is the technical component with the best Indian support, and every other part of the system depends on it. Across ten international indices, a HAR model of realized volatility extended with implied volatility and the overnight return beat every HAR variant without implied volatility ([Kambouroudis, McMillan & Tsakou 2021](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/fut.22241)). For Nifty specifically, forecasts built from realized volatility were "significantly more efficient and less biased" than the model-free implied volatility behind India VIX ([Garg & Vipul 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/fut.21680)), and high-low range estimators beat close-to-close history ([Vipul & Jacob 2007](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/fut.20283)). VIX should be an input to the forecast, not the forecast. The daily forecast is then split into a remaining-session variance using a time-of-day profile. That profile is reverse-J shaped on the NSE, and expiry days need their own version because their extra volatility arrives only in the last half-hour ([Agarwalla & Pandey 2013](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/fut.21632)). The few technical rules that survive testing share a recognisable profile: a regime condition, holds of 45–75 minutes or more, volatility-scaled bands, trades taken only on the strongest signals, a named flow mechanism, and low turnover.

### Options-market data prices the trade more than it predicts it

The volatility risk premium is real in India, and for a buyer it acts mainly as a cost and a timing rule. Short Nifty options earn significant returns overnight, while their open-to-close returns are "largely negative and insignificant". The premium is "mainly a reward for overnight risk" ([Bhat, Pandey & Nageswara Rao 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/fut.22512)). India VIX sat above the next 21 days' realized volatility on **80.5% of days** since 2015 ([own data](<../research_notes/04_nifty_measured_facts.md>)). Since the September 2025 expiry change, the realized open-to-close standard deviation has been only **0.69 of the VIX-implied daily move** ([own calculation](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/option_trade_construction.md>)). Two rules follow. Never carry a long option overnight. And buy only when the implied move left in the session is not rich compared with the HAR forecast, or when the model's expected move clears the straddle breakeven plus costs.

Dealer gamma is the most promising regime switch, and also the least settled for India. US and Chinese evidence agrees that net short gamma among liquidity providers goes with intraday momentum and higher realized volatility, while net long gamma goes with reversal and damping ([Dim, Eraker & Vilkov](https://dx.doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4692190)). The size is modest. Using actual Cboe market-maker positions, gamma hedging lowers volatility on average and raises daily realized volatility by at most **3.3 annualized points** ([Amaya et al. 2025](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/gammasqueezes.pdf)). The Indian problem is the sign. Indian GEX dashboards state openly that "dealer positioning is assumed, not measured" and copy the US convention ([draconic.ai](https://draconic.ai/tools/nifty-gamma-exposure)). With about 97% of individuals mainly buying both calls and puts ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)), the defensible Indian default is that liquidity providers are short both. That makes the size and strike concentration of gamma informative, rather than its sign. This can be settled now with free data. NSE's end-of-day bhavcopy gives strike-wise open interest and prices for every past expiry, so a daily gamma series can be rebuilt and tested against Nifty 1-minute futures under all three sign conventions.

Opening option flow has the best directional evidence in the options family, and it cannot be backtested here. In KOSPI 200 options, the signed imbalance of *out-of-the-money* trades in the first ten minutes predicted the rest-of-day index return with an **out-of-sample R² of 1.85%**. A simple timing rule kept **5.20 bps a day after costs (Sharpe 1.14)**, rising to **19.5 bps a day after costs** on the 127 days with signals beyond two standard deviations. The effect came from large traders, vanished when implied volatility was low, and did not appear in the 9:10–9:30 window ([Kang, Kang & Lee 2021](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/fut.22301)). India has no trade-initiator flag. The quote rule signs about 83% of option trades correctly and the tick rule only 59% ([Savickas & Wilson](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/on-inferring-the-direction-of-option-trades-savickas-robert/10005407236)). Historical option ticks are not available through SmartAPI either. So this signal starts as a shadow log on day one and can earn a vote only after several months of recorded data.

The popular Indian option-chain signals are the least supported. PCR built on volume has no predictive power for Nifty, and PCR built on open interest has only weak Granger causality ([MDPI 2019](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7099/7/1/24)). Pinning toward heavily traded strikes is a real mechanism in the US ([Golez & Jackwerth 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X12001365)), but Indian max-pain accuracy claims are untraceable. One logged test of 19 expiries found max pain predicted the close worse than assuming no change ([Stoxra](https://stoxra.com/blog/max-pain-theory-nifty-options-trading)). Since 3 August 2026, expiry settlement has come from the 15:15–15:35 closing auction. On 3 September 2026 the Sensex indicative level fell about 2.5% during the auction and put premiums jumped 400–500% ([MarketsEasy](https://marketseasy.in/blog/sebi-expiry-settlement-price-cas-review-2026)). Pre-2026 pinning history may not carry over. Open-interest walls are worth keeping only as expiry-day levels for targets and stops, after a free bhavcopy test against actual settlements.

### Macro and cross-asset inputs size the day, never its direction

Scheduled events change the expected range, and they mostly do not change it the way traders assume. Across 57 RBI decision days from 2016 to 2026, Nifty's range was **1.02 times what the prior VIX implied, which is no excess at all**, while India VIX fell 1.66% on the day before and **1.95% on the day itself (t = −3.81)**. Decision-day direction flipped sign between 2016–19 and 2020–26 ([own data](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/macro_crossasset_checks_output.txt>); RBI dates partly typed from memory). This matches older Indian evidence that VIX rises before announcements and falls after them ([Shaikh & Padhi 2013](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0973801013500168)). Equities react to the forward-guidance "path" surprise more than to the rate decision ([RBI WP 03/2024](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380127245_Equity_Markets_and_Monetary_Policy_Surprises_RBI_Working_Paper)). Buying options into an RBI decision therefore pays for volatility that then deflates. Budget days are different: their range ran **2.1 times implied** while VIX fell 9.3% on the day. The 4 June 2024 election result ran **6.2 times implied**, with VIX up 27.75% that day and down 29% the next ([own data](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/macro_crossasset_checks_output.txt>); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/market-commentary/story/election-results-2024-india-vix-jumps-india-alliance-exceeds-expectations-432101-2024-06-04)). India's CPI, IIP and GDP all come out at 16:00 IST, after the close, so they affect only the next morning's gap.

Overnight shocks set the size of the day but not its sign. VIX alone is well calibrated: the realized range over the implied move stays at 1.01–1.07 in every VIX bucket. Large overnight shocks add to it. A gap of 1–2% raised the ratio to **1.13**, a gap above 2% to **1.71**, a prior US move above 2.5% to 1.25, and a prior Brent move above 5% to 1.21 ([own data](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/macro_crossasset_checks_output.txt>)). These are in-sample bucket averages and should be shrunk toward 1.0 until they are re-checked. The 2026 oil regime has flipped a familiar sign. The correlation of the prior day's Brent move with the Nifty gap was **−0.27 in 2026, against +0.12 to +0.15 in earlier periods**, so an oil spike now means a gap-down. Even so, Brent never predicts the move after the open. Indian equities showed no significant response to FOMC surprises in the 49-country study by Hausman and Wongswan ([Fed IFDP 886](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/ifdp/2006/886/ifdp886.pdf)). The Indian session on FOMC day itself runs about 10% quieter than implied ([own data](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/macro_crossasset_checks_output.txt>)).

The slow macro variables belong in a stress score that raises the bar for trading. Geopolitical risk is risk-off for Nifty large caps at monthly horizons ([SenGupta & Debnath 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10368-026-00785-w)). Contractionary policy hurts Nifty returns and liquidity only when policy uncertainty is high ([Sahu, Debata & Sachdeva 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/irfi.70016)). October 2026 has both. The Fed hiked to 3.75–4.00% on 16 September ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/simonmoore/2026/09/29/what-to-expect-from-interest-rates-for-the-rest-of-2026/)), the RBI is widely expected to hike to 5.50% on 7 October ([HDFC Sky](https://hdfcsky.com/news/rbis-panel-starts-monetary-policy-deliberations-0-25-bps-rate-hike-likely)), and the 10-year G-sec is at 7.21% ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/india/government-bond-yield)). Valuation has no daily role. Classic equity-premium predictors mostly fail out of sample even in the US ([Goyal & Welch 2008](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhm014)). FII cash flows follow returns rather than lead them ([Patnaik, Shah & Singh 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2362.2013.12032.x)).

Two calendar patterns are strong enough to keep as small context features. Sessions after a weekday exchange holiday gap up by +0.18% and then fall **−0.22% from open to close (t = −2.96)**, and this holds in both 2015–19 and 2020–26. Since the September 2025 expiry swap ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/nse-bids-adieu-to-thursday-expiry-as-dates-swap-come-into-effect-explained-125082800635_1.html)), Nifty-expiry Tuesdays have averaged **−0.204% open-to-close and closed above their open only 27.8% of the time**. That is just 54 sessions out of many tests, and Tuesdays in 2015–19, before they were expiry days, were also weak (39.2% up), so the expiry story is not proven ([own data](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/macro_crossasset_checks_output.txt>)). Both patterns, together with the negative intraday drift and the spot-volatility asymmetry described below, lean against intraday call buying. They should enter the model as features it can price, not as triggers.

### Sentiment is a fuse, not a compass

Index-level text sentiment is too small and too reversible to pay an option buyer's costs. A one-standard-deviation rise in Wall Street Journal pessimism lowered the next day's Dow by **8.1 bps, and 6.8 bps of that reversed within the week** ([Tetlock 2007](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/3097/Tetlock_Media_Sentiment_JF.pdf)). The effect was 12 bps in recessions and 3.5 bps in expansions ([Garcia 2013](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12027)). That is 5–17% of Nifty's 0.70% average absolute daily move. The impressive LLM results come from cross-sections of single stocks, are concentrated in small stocks and bad news ([Lopez-Lira & Tang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619)), and fade quickly for large stocks ([Chen, Kelly & Xiu](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4416687)). For Nifty, topic sentiment from about 400,000 Economic Times articles improved level forecasts only marginally ([Dasgupta et al. 2024](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2412.06794)). Backtests of LLM sentiment are also contaminated by what the model remembers: one correction cut in-sample mean returns by **up to 67%** ([FINCAD 2026](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.24564)). Power settles the matter. Detecting a daily directional correlation of 0.05 at 80% power needs about 3,100 trading days, roughly 12 years ([own calculation](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/sentiment_signals.md>)), so a sentiment direction tilt cannot be validated within this project's lifetime.

Text works for volatility, and that defines its job. A newspaper-based equity-volatility tracker co-moves with VIX at about **0.8**, and kept that tracking out of sample through 2019–2023 ([Baker, Bloom, Davis & Kost](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25720/w25720.pdf)). The useful component is a cheap unscheduled-shock classifier. It deduplicates headlines, drops price-report headlines (which simply restate the lagged index move), tags category, surprise and severity, and blocks new entries for 15–30 minutes after a severity-3 unscheduled shock while implied volatility reprices. Intraday breadth of the index constituents, such as the weighted share rising since the open and the share above VWAP, is the other inexpensive confirmation. It has no Indian evidence yet, but 1-minute constituent history makes it testable. Social media should not be built. SEBI's January 2025 rules confine "education" to prices at least three months old, so live public calls are either non-compliant or noise ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-finfluencer-circular-live-stock-data-market-education-rules-125013000571_1.html)). The pipeline costs under about ₹300 a month on the cheapest LLM tier. One practical trap: Moneycontrol's market RSS feeds still return HTTP 200, but their newest items date from April 2024 ([feed](https://www.moneycontrol.com/rss/marketreports.xml)). Use ET, Business Standard, Mint, NSE and RBI feeds, and time-stamp every item by when your own poller first saw it.

## A ₹500 stop sits inside ordinary Nifty noise

The geometry of fixed barriers drives everything that follows. If the index has no drift and you set a target *a* above entry and a stop *b* below, the chance of touching the target first is **b/(a+b)**, whatever the volatility. Only a real forecast, meaning drift, moves that number. Overpriced implied volatility changes how long the trade takes and how much theta it costs, but not the ratio. At a target of 0.23% and a stop of 0.11%, the no-edge probability is 0.324, and the expected time to resolve is only about 22 minutes. Converting hourly forecast accuracy into barrier odds gives **0.36 at 55% accuracy, 0.40 at 60% and 0.44 at 65%** ([own calculation](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/option_trade_construction.md>)). Even a strong forecast barely moves the barrier odds. That is the structural reason why "55% accuracy" is the wrong target. Honest out-of-sample models reach 52–58% on daily index direction. One walk-forward SPY study scored 58.45% overall and 73% only on the 154 of 799 days with large predicted moves ([Chen 2026](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.26106)). The accuracy this system needs therefore exists, if anywhere, only on a selected minority of days.

The rupee cap fixes the stop distance, and that distance is noise. One lot's rupee sensitivity is delta × lot × index level. A Nifty option with delta 0.3 moves about ₹19.5 per index point, so a ₹500 loss, after roughly ₹100 of theta and exit slippage, buys about 20 points, or 0.09% of 22,556. On open-anchored data for 2024–2026, the median day's range was 0.84%, and the index moved at least 0.11% against its open on **85% of days** ([own calculation](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/option_trade_construction.md>)). A stop this tight asks "is the timing right over the next 20 minutes?" It does not ask "is the forecast wrong?" That is acceptable only for entries where a structural invalidation, such as the noise-band edge or VWAP, sits within the stop. Theory points the same way: stop-losses lower expected return under a random walk and help only when returns trend after the stop is hit ([Kaminski & Lo 2014](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf)). The positive-gamma, mean-reverting regime is where tight stops get run, which is another reason to gate entries on the trend regime.

### What ₹5,000 can buy, day by day

Pricing weekly options correctly needs a business-time clock. India VIX is a 30-day, calendar-time measure, and plugging it into Black-76 with calendar time prices an at-the-money (ATM) Nifty option at 9:30 on expiry day at about ₹35 per unit. The business-time value, which treats the trading session as carrying most of the day's variance, is about ₹66, so a calendar-time backtest **understates the entry cost by roughly half** and overstates leverage ([own calculation](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/option_trade_construction.md>)). With that clock, at VIX near 14.7, a full ATM lot costs ₹4,291 at 9:30 and ₹3,742 at 11:00 on a Nifty expiry Tuesday, and ₹6,973 one day before expiry. Sensex costs ₹3,878 at 9:30 on its expiry Thursday and ₹6,527 the day before. The budget rule (premium × lot + ₹60 ≤ ₹5,000) caps premiums at about **₹75 per unit for Nifty and ₹245 for Sensex**. So each weekday has a natural instrument.

| Day | Nifty (Tue expiry) | Sensex (Thu expiry) | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | 1 day to expiry: 0.4–0.9% out of the money (delta 0.23–0.36) | 3 days: far OTM only | Nifty, 1 day to expiry |
| Tue | Expiry day: ATM fits before ~11:00 | 2 days: far OTM | Nifty, expiry day |
| Wed | Next week: far OTM | 1 day: 0.3–0.7% OTM (delta 0.27–0.40) | Sensex, 1 day to expiry |

| Fri | 2 sessions + weekend: 0.6–1.1% OTM | Next week: far OTM | Nifty, or skip |

Theta and implied volatility then work against the position from the moment of entry. With the index unchanged from 9:15, an ATM expiry-day option keeps about **85% of its value at 11:00, 63% at 13:00 and 40% at 14:30**. A 0.6% OTM option keeps only 64%, 24% and 2%. A 0.42% OTM Nifty call bought at 10:00 for ₹1,608 a lot loses about ₹625 by noon with the index flat, so **a ₹500 premium stop is triggered by time decay alone after about 1 hour 40 minutes** ([own calculation](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/option_trade_construction.md>)). India VIX rises about 0.79 points per 1% Nifty fall and falls about 0.46 per 1% rise (2018–2026; 1.12 and 0.57 in 2025–26), consistent with peer-reviewed evidence of a negative, asymmetric Nifty return–volatility relation ([Pati, Rajib & Barai 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rfe.2017.07.004)). That cushions put buyers and taxes call buyers. The larger risk is a shift in the *level* of implied volatility. A two-point drop cuts that 0.42% OTM call's target gain from ₹840 to ₹366. The pricing model therefore needs a calibrated level ratio by days-to-expiry and time of day, plus skew and the asymmetric spot–volatility beta, fitted from two to four weeks of your own chain snapshots. The acceptance test: median model error at the strikes the picker would choose below ₹35 a lot.

### Costs, stops and the strike that maximizes expected value

Fixed charges are small per trade but large against the risk budget. At Angel One, one lot round trip costs about **₹56–58** in brokerage (₹20 an order), STT at 0.15% of the sell premium since April 2026, exchange charges (0.0355% NSE, 0.0325% BSE), the SEBI fee, stamp duty and 18% GST ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/exchange-transaction-charges); [ICICI Direct](https://www.icicidirect.com/futures-and-options/articles/stt-changes-in-budget-2026-what-f-o-traders-need-to-know)). Brokerage plus its GST is ₹47 of that. The spread comes on top: a one-tick Nifty spread costs ₹6.5 a round trip, for an all-in ≈₹63, and a ₹0.50 Sensex half-spread costs ₹20, for ≈₹76. Stop exits in fast markets cost two to three times more. That ₹57 alone is 1.1% of capital and 11% of a ₹500 stop. In US 0DTE options, roughly 60% of retail's daily losses were transaction costs, and effective spreads ran 45–80% of the mid-price beyond 2% out of the money ([Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)). One cautionary tale applies directly to the backtest code: a published 0DTE strategy paper reversed its conclusion after a replication found a unit bug that charged the half-spread at 1/100 of its true size ([Vilkov repo](https://github.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies)). Unit-test the cost model against a real Angel One contract note.

Stop orders behave differently from what most retail traders assume. Stop-loss market orders for options were withdrawn on NSE in September 2021 ([Zerodha bulletin](https://zerodha.com/marketintel/bulletin/305785/stop-loss-market-sl-m-orders-blocked-for-index-options)) and on BSE in October 2023 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/bse-to-discontinue-stop-loss-market-orders-from-oct-9-to-check-freak-trades-123092200889_1.html)). Only stop-limit orders exist, and they can fail to fill in a gap. The right design is a primary stop on the *index level*, executed from the alert as a marketable limit order, plus a broker-side stop-limit on the premium as a catastrophe backstop. Set its trigger at entry minus ₹550 a lot (₹8.5 a unit for Nifty, ₹27.5 for Sensex), with the limit a further 10% of premium or five ticks below so it still fills in a fast market. Add a time stop on top.

Strike choice follows from fixed per-lot costs. Expected value per strike is p × gain − (1 − p) × loss − costs, and the gain/loss ratio improves toward the money, so **with any real edge, expected value is highest at the highest delta the loss cap allows**. Simulation on 20,000 paths, entering at 10:00 on a Nifty expiry day with a 0.23% target and 0.11% stop, makes the trade-off concrete ([own calculation](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/option_trade_construction.md>)).

| Strike (cost per lot) | Net at stop | Net at target | EV, no edge (p = 0.34) | EV, modest edge (p = 0.38) | EV, strong edge (p = 0.42) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 22550 ATM (₹4,117) | −₹1,119 | +₹1,881 | −₹92 | +₹34 | +₹153 |
| 22600 (₹2,660) | −₹861 | +₹1,383 | −₹92 | +₹3 | +₹92 |
| 22650, 0.42% OTM (₹1,608) | −₹620 | +₹930 | −₹89 | −₹23 | +₹39 |
| 22700 (₹905) | −₹420 | +₹563 | −₹83 | −₹41 | −₹2 |
| 22750 (₹473) | −₹273 | +₹297 | −₹77 | −₹53 | −₹30 |

The table explains the whole design. With these barriers, the only expiry-day strike inside the ₹500 cap (22700) **does not break even even with a strong forecast**. The ATM strike makes money but breaks the cap, and cheaper strikes lose at every edge level. Below delta 0.2, fixed costs dominate and expected value is negative at every edge tested. The trade becomes viable only when the structural stop is tighter (0.08–0.10%), so that a delta 0.30–0.40 strike fits the cap. The index target/stop ratio then has to reach 2.3–2.6 to yield roughly ₹800 against ₹500 after theta, because theta compresses a 2.1× index ratio into only 1.4–1.7× in option rupees. At those barriers the no-edge probability falls to about 0.29, so the forecast must supply even more lift.

Spreads would help, and they are out of reach. US retail spreads had median returns of +3.0% to +3.3% against −0.7% to −0.8% for single legs ([Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)). In India, though, the short leg draws an exposure margin of 2% of contract value ([INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/learn/fno/span-and-exposure-margin)): **about ₹29,300 for one Nifty lot and ₹29,000 for one Sensex lot**, with an extra 2% on expiry day. Traders report spread margins of ₹14,000 or more even when the maximum loss is about ₹1,250 ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/margin-required-on-call-spreads-looks-too-high/174263)). Spreads should be revisited at roughly ₹50,000 of capital, after checking Angel One's calculator. Until then, the system buys single legs only: near the money, intraday, closed the same session, never in the last hour of an expiry day and never into the closing auction. Indian evidence is blunt about the alternative: day-trading profits in index options are "flat to weakly positive", while positions not closed the same day lose significantly ([Agarwal, Ghosh, Prabhala & Zhao](https://www.fma.org/assets/docs/Derivatives2025/Zhao.pdf)).

## Three planning assumptions fail the arithmetic

The ₹5,000 budget squeezes the design from both sides. A larger rupee risk breaks Kelly and invites ruin. A smaller one either forces lottery-ticket strikes or makes fixed costs dominate. Neither problem can be engineered away at this capital level, so the plan should say so in numbers.

| Assumption | What the numbers show | Revised planning value |
|---|---|---|
| Risk ₹200–500 per trade on ₹5,000 | 4–10% of capital per trade. Full Kelly, net of 0.1R costs at +1.9R/−1.1R payoffs, is 1.9% at p = 0.38, 4.8% at 0.40 and 7.7% at 0.42, so ₹500 is within full Kelly only if p ≳ 0.44. Over 120 trades with a genuine 42% hit rate, ₹500 risk gives a 42% chance of falling below ₹2,500 and 22% of being unable to place the next trade; ₹200 risk cuts these to 12% and 1%. But a ₹200 stop is about 8 Nifty points, which no structural stop fits | Hard cap ₹500 including stop slippage, planned around ₹450; treat ₹5,000 as experiment capital; ~₹13,000–15,000 makes ₹500 about half-Kelly at p = 0.42; ₹50,000 makes it the 1% rule and opens spreads |
| Breakeven 35–38% at a 1:2 payoff | Breakeven = (loss + costs)/(gain + loss). At ₹1,000 / ₹500 it is 38% with ₹70 all-in costs, 40% at ₹100 and 43% at ₹150; at ₹800 / ₹500 it is 44%, 46% and 50%. 35–38% holds only if costs and slippage stay under ~₹70, essentially fees alone | Plan on 42–44% until fills are measured; gate trades at measured breakeven + 0.04 |
| Target ₹800–1,000 against ≤₹500 loss | At 0.11%/0.23% barriers, no expiry-day Nifty strike delivers both; theta turns a 2.1× index ratio into 1.4–1.7× in rupees | Replace with net gain/loss ≥ 1.5 and index target/stop ≥ 2.3; accept that average EV is tens of rupees |
| Delta 0.2–0.6 | Below 0.2, EV is negative at every edge; above ~0.55, a lot is unaffordable except on expiry day | Delta 0.25–0.55, choosing the highest delta that fits the cap |
| 8–12 weeks of paper trading, ~40 signals | After 40 trades, a true 42% system shows ≤37% about 23% of the time, and a no-edge 36% system shows ≥42% about 24% of the time; the 95% interval around an observed 42% is 27–59%. A fixed test of 36% vs 42% needs ~400 trades | Paper phase checks mechanics and costs only; live go/no-go by sequential test, ~95–115 trades on average and ~190–225 at the 90th percentile, i.e. 7–9 months at three trades a week |

Sources for the table: Kelly, ruin, breakeven and power figures are simulations and closed-form calculations in the project notes ([fusion and validation](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/signal_fusion_validation.md>); [trade construction](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/option_trade_construction.md>)). The sequential-test figures were recomputed for this report.

The ruin numbers come from streaks, not averages. In those 120-trade simulations, the median longest losing streak was **7 trades, and 11 at the 90th percentile**, even with a real 42% edge. Seven ₹500 losses would take ₹3,500 from a ₹5,000 account. The account also shrinks the menu as it falls. After two losses, ₹4,000 no longer buys the morning ATM expiry-day lot that the strike table favours, so the system is pushed toward the lower-delta strikes that lose money at every edge level. That is why a drawdown pause at about −30% (₹3,500) makes more sense here than the −40% a generic rule would give. Below that level the system is no longer trading the strategy it was validated on.

Tightening the stop does not fix breakeven, because the payoff ratio and the no-edge hit probability move together. A ₹200 stop against an ₹800 target looks like a 27% breakeven. But a 7–8-point index stop against a 40-point target has a no-edge hit probability of about b/(a+b) ≈ 0.15, before one-minute jitter is counted. Every rupee ratio you can pick is offset by its barrier probability, and only forecast drift creates an edge. Costs and theta then subtract from it. A 55% hit rate is therefore not the target. The measurable target is **calibrated P(target before stop) minus the measured breakeven, which must exceed about four percentage points**, and it appears only on a minority of days.

Paper trading cannot carry the statistical burden in your timeline. A Wald sequential probability ratio test (SPRT) with deliberately wide hypotheses, breakeven against breakeven plus eight points (α = 0.10, β = 0.20), decides fastest ([Wald 1945](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177731118)). If measured breakeven is 0.42, the rule is **GO once wins ≥ 6.44 + 0.46 × trades and STOP once wins ≤ −4.66 + 0.46 × trades**. At 40 trades that means at least 25 wins to accept and at most 13 to reject; at 100 trades, 53 and 41. In simulation it decides after about 94 trades on average when there is no edge and about 113 when there is, with a 90th percentile near 190–225. At a maximum of three trades a week, the realistic go/no-go comes in 7–9 months, not 8–12 weeks. The legitimate speed-up is to score calibration and hit rate on *every labelled setup*, taken or not, about 23 a month, because barrier outcomes come from market data. Cost and slippage still need real fills.

## Few weights, calibrated odds and a fixed trial budget

With about 2,000 historical setups and a weak signal, fusion should estimate as few weights as possible. When forecasters are noisy and samples small, simple and shrunk combinations beat estimated "optimal" weights, because the weights themselves carry estimation error ([Timmermann 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1574-0706(05)01004-9); [Smith & Wallis 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0084.2008.00541.x)). Combining individually weak predictors gave significant out-of-sample gains for the US equity premium where single predictors failed ([Rapach, Strauss & Zhou 2010](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhp063)), and technical and macro information complement each other ([Neely, Rapach, Tu & Zhou 2014](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2013.1838)). The recipe is to turn each family into one sign-aligned score. A positive score means the family agrees with the proposed side; scores are winsorized at ±3 and standardized only on the training window. The baseline is an equal-weight consensus of those scores. The learned model is a ridge-penalized logistic regression with one weight per family, not one per indicator. The regime enters as one feature with at most one interaction, because separate per-regime models would split the sample below any defensible minimum. Sentiment enters only as a veto.

Model choice follows from sample size, not fashion. In 145 clinical comparisons at low risk of bias, the AUC difference between machine learning and logistic regression was **0.00** ([Christodoulou et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2019.02.004)). Random forests and neural nets needed more than ten times as many events per variable to stabilize ([van der Ploeg, Austin & Steyerberg 2014](https://bmcmedresmethodol.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2288-14-137)). Riley's criterion for keeping overfitting to 10% ([Riley et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.7992)), at a realistic out-of-sample AUC of 0.58 and a 38% positive rate, requires about **1,550 setups for 3 parameters and 2,580 for 5**, and 3,670 for 3 parameters at AUC 0.55 ([own calculation](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/signal_fusion_validation.md>)). So with about 2,000 setups the meta-model gets three to six effective parameters. It should be **one pooled model across all setups**, with the setup as a feature. Gradient boosting may run only as a pre-registered challenger: depth 2, monotone constraints, recalibrated, and adopted only if it beats logistic regression by at least 1% relative log-loss with no worse calibration. Meta-labeling, the pattern of a rule choosing the side and a model choosing take-or-skip, is sound but thinly evidenced. In the one real-data out-of-sample case, precision rose only from **0.17 to 0.20** ([Singh & Joubert](https://hudsonthames.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Does-Meta-Labeling-Add-to-Signal-Efficacy.pdf)). Its large "accuracy" gains mostly count trades correctly skipped.

Calibration is the whole game, because the expected-value gate multiplies it. With a 2:1 payoff, each percentage point of overconfidence overstates expected value by 0.03R, so **a model that says 0.45 when the truth is 0.40 claims an edge of about 0.15R, roughly the entire edge of a 42% system** ([own calculation](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/signal_fusion_validation.md>)). Logistic regression is close to calibrated by construction. Boosted trees push probabilities away from 0 and 1. Platt scaling suits small calibration sets, and isotonic regression needs more data ([Niculescu-Mizil & Caruana 2005](https://doi.org/10.1145/1102351.1102430)), above roughly 1,000 points by scikit-learn's guidance ([scikit-learn](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html)). With 2,000 out-of-fold predictions in five quantile bins of about 400 each, calibration can be checked only to about ±5 points. That is why the gate requires a calibrated probability at least **four points above measured breakeven**. Never rebalance classes in a model whose probability feeds the gate, and judge it on log-loss, Brier skill, and calibration intercept and slope, with AUC secondary.

Labels and splits must respect decision time. Each setup gets a triple-barrier label: target, stop or time limit, whichever comes first ([López de Prado 2018](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Advances+in+Financial+Machine+Learning-p-9781119482086)). Barriers are set on the index or futures in units of remaining-session volatility, never on synthetic premiums. Because every label closes the same day, purging means grouping folds by trading day, with an embargo of at least five trading days, or the longest feature lookback if that is longer. Development on 2019–2023 should use combinatorial purged cross-validation with ten half-year groups and two test groups, giving 45 splits and 9 backtest paths. In synthetic tests this controlled false discoveries better than walk-forward ([Arian, Norouzi & Seco 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950705124011110)). After that comes a frozen walk-forward on 2024–2025 and one look at 2026. Lot size, STT, expiry weekday and settlement rules must be those in force on each date. Features must use days-to-expiry rather than weekday names. The leakage to fear is decision-time leakage: one 2026 paper's "71%" accuracy came from predicting close-versus-prior-close after the open was already known ([Chen 2026](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.26106)). Entry is at the next bar plus a manual delay of 0, 1, 3 and 5 minutes, and any signal whose sign flips at three minutes is rejected.

Every configuration tried raises the bar for the one you keep. The deflated Sharpe ratio corrects for the best of N trials ([Bailey & López de Prado 2014](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf)). For this system's plausible edge, a per-trade Sharpe of about 0.13 (p ≈ 0.43 at +1.9R/−1.1R), the deflated Sharpe on 600 out-of-sample trades stays near 0.95 only if **about ten or fewer effectively independent configurations** were tried. It drops to 0.87 at 30 and 0.75 at 100 ([own calculation](<../research_notes/Nifty options multifactor quant strategy/signal_fusion_validation.md>)). The same edge needs about 245 trades for t ≥ 2 and 550 for t ≥ 3, the threshold Harvey, Liu and Zhu recommend once multiple testing is acknowledged ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv059)). The practical tool is an append-only experiment registry from day one. One row per run records the code commit, data hash, hypothesis, parameters, out-of-sample metrics, cumulative trial count and a cluster ID, with a programme-wide budget of **10–15 effective configurations** and a probability of backtest overfitting at or below 0.2 ([Bailey et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.21314/JCF.2016.322)).

The data you can get shapes what can be validated historically. SmartAPI gives live chains but no expired-option history and no historical intraday open interest. Index and futures signals can be backtested on 2019–2026 1-minute futures. Daily gamma, open-interest walls and daily VRP can be backtested from free bhavcopy. Option-flow, intraday skew and intraday term-structure signals can only be shadow-logged. Option profit and loss in the backtest is therefore a model output until it is checked against recorded chains, or against one paid source of 1-minute expired-option data if the budget allows. If the median error in the modelled premium *change* over the holding period exceeds about 15%, translate index-barrier outcomes through recorded deltas instead. Once live, drift is slow to detect at this trade rate. A Bernoulli CUSUM ([Page 1954](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/41.1-2.100); [Reynolds & Stoumbos 1999](https://doi.org/10.1080/00224065.1999.11979900)) with threshold 2.5 raises a false alarm about once every 600 trades and needs about 100 trades to flag a fall from 42% to 33%. It has to sit alongside a hard capital stop. Refits should happen every six months or after 250 new labelled setups, whichever is later. Each refit averages an expanding-window model with a three-year rolling model, since no single window wins under structural breaks ([Pesaran & Timmermann 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2006.03.010)).

The revision keeps the draft's skeleton: a day card, an opening read, index-anchored barriers, a strike picker and a confidence number. It changes what each part is allowed to do.

### A trading day, step by step

The evening before, after about 19:00, the system computes the slow inputs: FII participant positioning, FII/DII flows, India 10-year and US 10-year five-day changes, the weekly geopolitical risk reading, the event calendar, and the daily jump-model regime and HAR volatility forecast. At 08:45 it drafts the day card, and at about 09:05 it finalizes it from the GIFT Nifty-implied gap. The card states whether trading is allowed and from when. Election-result days are no-trade. Budget day allows entries only after the speech, at a higher hurdle and with a 2× range. RBI day allows no entries until 30–45 minutes after the decision. On index-rebalance days, positions are flat by 15:00. The card also gives the expected range: VIX-implied daily move × spot × 1.04 × event multiplier × shock multiplier, with shock multipliers shrunk toward 1.0 until re-validated. It gives the instrument of the day from the affordability calendar, the stress score, and the context features (post-holiday, expiry day, gap fade, oil-regime sign).

With one lot there is no half size, so the stress score's size cut becomes a higher bar. The default is a margin above breakeven of +0.04 when the stress score is 2 or less, +0.06 at 3–4 and +0.08 at 5 or more, with thresholds tuned so the top tier fires on 10–15% of days. These are defaults to calibrate, not tested values. From 9:15 to 9:25 the logger records option ticks for strikes ATM ± 10 on both weeklies and computes the signed OTM imbalance for the shadow log. From 9:45 to 13:30 on expiry days, and to 14:30 otherwise, the system checks the breakout trigger every 15 minutes. On non-expiry days it makes a single late-day check at 14:45 once that setup is cleared. All positions are out by **15:00 on expiry days**, ahead of the 15:15 closing auction, and by 15:10 on other days. After the close it stores every setup, taken or not, with its features, probability, market-data label, and quotes and fills.

### Gates, fusion and the alert

An alert fires only when every gate passes in sequence. The event and shock gates come first. There must be no unscheduled severity-3 headline in the last 15–30 minutes, and intraday VIX must not have moved more than about ±8% (a starting value). A setup trigger then names the side and the index stop and target. Next comes the stop-fit filter: the structural invalidation (band edge or VWAP) must be within the stop budget, and the target must be at least 2.3 times the stop. The room-to-run filter requires no strong opposing level, such as a round 100 on Nifty, the prior-day high or low, or a multi-touch session extreme, closer than the target. The volatility gate requires that the remaining-session implied move is not rich against the HAR forecast. The confirmation gates require that constituent breadth agrees with the side and that five-level order-flow imbalance over the last 3–5 minutes is not more than one standard deviation against it.

The meta-model then estimates P(target before stop before deadline) from at most nine columns, with ridge shrinkage holding effective degrees of freedom near four to six. The columns are two setup dummies, technical, options-implied and context family scores, one regime feature (jump-model state or HAR percentile, plus dealer gamma once validated), and one days-to-expiry or time-of-day feature. The expected-value gate requires EV ≥ +0.10R after measured costs, the probability at least the stress-adjusted margin above measured breakeven, and Kelly not exceeded, meaning f* = p − (1 − p) × loss/gain ≥ loss/capital. The alert shows the calibrated probability, the no-edge odds b/(a+b), measured breakeven, EV in rupees, which families agreed, and the reason. A trigger that fails any gate is logged as a skipped setup, which feeds calibration at no cost.

### Strike picker, exits and kill-switches

The picker prices every candidate: Nifty and Sensex, this week's and next week's expiry, calls for long signals and puts for short ones, from one strike in the money to 1.5% out. Pricing uses the calibrated business-time model with skew and the asymmetric spot–volatility beta, rounded to the tick. It keeps a strike only if premium × lot + ₹60 fits the capital, loss at the stop including three half-spreads of exit slippage is at most ₹500, net gain/loss is at least 1.5, delta is between 0.25 and 0.55, and the quoted spread is no wider than two ticks or 1.5% of mid for Nifty (four ticks or 2% for Sensex). Bid and ask must each show at least five lots, and expected value must reach at least ₹75. It picks the highest-EV survivor, breaking ties on the cheaper rupee spread; if nothing survives, the answer is no trade. Orders go in as a limit at mid plus one tick, cancelled after 60 seconds and never chased more than two ticks. Straight after the fill, the trader places the stop-limit backstop and a resting target limit.

The trade exits on whichever comes first: the index touching the stop (sell at bid minus two ticks), the index touching the target, a VWAP cross against the position, a stall in which the index sits within ±0.05% of entry after 45 minutes, the 90-minute deadline, or the hard session exit. Nothing is held overnight. While capital is under ₹10,000, the system takes at most **one trade a day and three a week**, which also keeps turnover low, the variable SEBI found most closely tied to losses. Trading pauses on whichever kill-switch fires first: the SPRT stop line, a CUSUM alarm (starting from the validated win rate and alarming at nine points below it, threshold 2.5), capital below ₹3,500, or live costs more than 30% above the backtest assumption. Alerts-only use at this frequency stays far below SEBI's 10-orders-a-second threshold for algorithm registration ([Zerodha In the Money](https://inthemoneybyzerodha.substack.com/p/sebi-algo-trading-changes-april-2026)).

### Rollout, and what this week looks like

The first month is measurement, not trading. The logger records 1-minute chain snapshots for ATM ± 10 strikes on both weeklies, ticks from 9:15 to 9:30, the index, futures and VIX, plus the news log, so that the pricing ratio, skew, spreads and real costs can be calibrated within two to four weeks. Four free-data tests run in parallel. The first is the rest-of-day momentum regression on 1-minute Nifty futures, conditioned on daily gamma rebuilt from bhavcopy under three sign conventions, with breaks at September 2025 and August 2026. The second tests expiry-eve open-interest walls and max pain against actual settlements. The third is the noise-band breakout with option costs and delays. The fourth fits the HAR volatility engine. Historical validation then follows the registry, purged cross-validation, walk-forward and one-shot 2026 holdout protocol above. A paper phase of at least 10 weeks and 40 alerts tests mechanics. It passes with zero unresolved defects, measured all-in cost no more than 20% above assumption, and mean predicted probability within eight points of the observed rate across all labelled setups. Live trading with one lot follows under the SPRT. Shadow features such as opening option flow get a single pre-registered trial after about 300 live labelled setups, roughly 12–14 months.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question the system answers. It no longer asks "which way will Nifty go today?" It asks "is today one of the few days when a measured, regime-confirmed move is cheap enough to buy inside a ₹500 budget?" The evidence supports that question far better than the first, and the main levers are measurement, not prediction: a business-time pricing model, a measured cost and spread table, a remaining-session volatility forecast, and a calibrated probability tested against measured breakeven. Two free experiments decide whether any directional edge exists: the dealer-gamma momentum test on rebuilt bhavcopy data, and the noise-band backtest on 1-minute futures. If both fail, the honest system is one that never fires, and against an 87.7% loss base rate that is still a valuable result.

The less comfortable implication is that capital, not cleverness, is now the tightest constraint. At ₹5,000, every structurally sound trade risks more than Kelly allows, and two losses change which contracts the system can afford. At around ₹15,000 the same ₹500 risk becomes roughly half-Kelly, and at ₹50,000 defined-risk spreads become available. The most useful framing is to treat the ₹5,000 phase as paid measurement, with three outputs: verified costs, a calibrated model, and a sequential-test verdict after about 100 trades. Profit is not one of them. This is research for a system you will build and run yourself. It is not investment advice or a recommendation to trade any contract.
